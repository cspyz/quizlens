Reading Comprehension · Urban Science

The Quiet Work of Urban Trees

Original text written for this practice page · B2–C1 level

Stand on a street lined with lindens on a July afternoon, then walk one block to a street with nothing but asphalt, and you can feel the difference before you can explain it: five degrees cooler, perhaps more. Part of that relief is shade, which keeps sunlight from reaching the pavement at all. The rest comes from the leaves themselves. Water drawn up through the roots escapes as vapour through tiny pores, and every gram that evaporates carries heat away with it. A single mature tree can do the work of several small air conditioners, and it does so without a power cord.

Temperature is only the beginning. Leaves are slightly rough and faintly sticky surfaces, and dust, pollen and diesel soot settle on them; when it rains, much of that load is washed to the ground and away. The canopy also slows falling water, so that it reaches the soil in drops rather than in a torrent, and roots and leaf litter hold it there. In a city where a heavy storm can fill the drains in twenty minutes, a row of trees along the curb works as a quiet kind of plumbing. One study of a mid-sized American city estimated that its street trees intercepted hundreds of millions of litres of rainfall a year.

And yet the life of a street tree is not an easy one. The soil it stands in is often a compacted remnant, squeezed by decades of feet and tyres until water can barely enter. In winter it is salted; in summer it is asked to survive on whatever rain reaches it, because the budget that paid for the sapling rarely covers the watering can. Roots are cut for pipes, branches for power lines, trunks for parking spaces. A tree in a forest may live for a century; the same species in a narrow city pit is often replaced every fifteen or twenty years, and each removal costs more than the planting that came before it.

None of this is an argument against planting. It is an argument for planning. Cities that have made progress — Melbourne, Singapore, Rotterdam — tend to do the same three things: they give roots real volume to grow in, they plant a mix of species so that one pest cannot erase a whole avenue, and they treat the trees as infrastructure with a maintenance schedule rather than as decoration with a ceremony. The results are not dramatic in any single season. That is rather the point. A street tree is a piece of public equipment designed to run for eighty years, and the only fair way to judge it is over that span.

3. The word “intercepted” in Paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to ___.
4. Why does the writer mention “the budget that paid for the sapling”?
5. What can be inferred from Paragraph 3?
6. What is the writer's main purpose?

  1. B
  2. C
  3. A（intercept = 拦截、截住，使水未流失先被拦下）
  4. D
  5. C

Reading Comprehension · Narrative

Eleven Minutes

Original text written for this practice page · B2 level

Mira counted the till for the third time, and the numbers still refused to agree. By the time she found the mistake — a ten-yuan note folded in with the receipts — the clock above the oven said 23:12. The last train left at 23:40. She pulled on her coat, called a goodbye to nobody in particular, and ran.

The street was nearly empty. A bus sighed past without stopping. She reached the station with four minutes to spare and took the stairs two at a time. Halfway along the platform an old man ahead of her stumbled, and a small black case swung out of his hand and struck the tiles. The catch sprang open. Sheet music slid out in a white fan, and the man stood looking down at it as though he had forgotten what hands were for.

Mira looked at the clock. Then she knelt and began gathering pages, chasing one that the draft from the tunnel kept nudging toward the edge. The old man crouched beside her, saying thank you in a language she did not know and did not need. Behind them the train doors chimed once, twice, and closed.

“You missed it,” he said, in careful English. It was not a question.

“There's another in twenty minutes,” Mira said, though she knew there was not.

He opened the case properly then and lifted out the violin. “Then I will play for you,” he said, “so that you are not late for nothing.”

He played for eleven minutes. The platform emptied and filled and emptied again. A cleaner leaned on her cart and did not move. When the last train finally came, Mira got on with the tune still in her head, and the strange, steady happiness of someone who has lost twenty minutes and been paid in something else.

Choose the best answer for each question.

1. Why was Mira in a hurry?
2. What made her miss the train?
3. The sentence “the man stood looking down at it as though he had forgotten what hands were for” suggests that ___.
4. Why does the writer add “It was not a question”?
5. What does the last paragraph suggest about Mira?
6. Which would be the best title for the story?
参考答案
  1. A
  2. B
  3. A
  4. A
  5. C
  6. B

改语句 · Sentence transformation

10 items

Rewrite each sentence as required, keeping the meaning unchanged. Write the whole new sentence.

1. The storm destroyed the bridge.
改为被动语态：The bridge ___
2. “I will call you tomorrow,” she said.
改为间接引语（注意人称、时态和时间状语）
3. The box was so heavy that I couldn't lift it.
用 too … to 改写
4. He finished his homework. Then he went out for a walk.
用 After 开头合并成一句（可加 -ing 或完成式分词）
5. Although it rained heavily, they continued the game.
用 In spite of 改写
6. I have never seen such a beautiful sunset.
改为倒装句（否定词提前）
7. “Don't touch the wire,” the technician said to us.
用 warn sb. not to do sth. 改写
8. If you don't hurry, you will miss the train.
用 Unless 改写
9. The girl is my sister. She is playing the piano.
用定语从句合并成一句
10. He is too young to drive a car.
用 so … that 改写
参考答案
  1. The bridge was destroyed by the storm.
  2. She said (that) she would call me the next day.
  3. The box was too heavy for me to lift.
  4. After finishing his homework, he went out for a walk.（或 After he had finished his homework, … / Having finished his homework, …）
  5. In spite of the heavy rain, they continued the game.
  6. Never have I seen such a beautiful sunset.
  7. The technician warned us not to touch the wire.
  8. Unless you hurry, you will miss the train.
  9. The girl who is playing the piano is my sister.
  10. He is so young that he cannot drive a car.

改句答案不唯一，只要结构正确、意思不变即可。

改错 · Error correction

6 items

Each sentence contains one error. Rewrite the sentence correctly.

1. She suggested to go to the cinema.
2. The number of students in our school are increasing.
3. I look forward to hear from you soon.
4. He has been married with a doctor for ten years.
5. Neither of the answers were correct.
6. If I will have time, I will join you.
参考答案
  1. She suggested going to the cinema.（suggest doing sth.）
  2. The number of students in our school is increasing.（the number of … 谓语用单数）
  3. I look forward to hearing from you soon.（look forward to doing sth.）
  4. He has been married to a doctor for ten years.（be married to sb.）
  5. Neither of the answers was correct.（neither of … 谓语用单数）
  6. If I have time, I will join you.（条件状语从句用一般现在时表将来）
